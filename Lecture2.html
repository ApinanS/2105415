Lecture 2 · Overview

Design basis = the information you must fix before you design

Motivation

Every number you assume turns into steel, energy and money

A wrong design basis cannot be fixed later by better calculation — it is amplified by every step that follows.
  • Capacity fixes reactor and column size.
  • Product specification fixes separation difficulty.
  • Raw material price fixes profitability — often 60–80 % of production cost.
  • Site and utility data fix operability in the real climate.
Rule: write every assumption down, with its source and date. That document is the design basis.

Lecture 2-1 · 1. Specifications

“Benzene” is not a specification

Two plants buying “benzene” can be buying two different products at two different prices.
  • Industrial grade / bulk — the cheap commodity stream, loose impurity limits.
  • Application grade — purity fixed by the downstream user (e.g. benzene for cyclohexane feedstock).
A specification is only complete when every line has a limit and a test method (e.g. ASTM D-4492).

Why are two columns given instead of one average?
  • The upstream isomerisation catalyst ages, so the feed composition drifts over the run.
  • Equipment must work at both ends — design for the worst case of each item, not the average.
  • Trace limits (S, Cl, water) are catalyst-poison limits, not quality limits.

Lecture 2-1 · 2. Storage T and P

Same chemical, two completely different tanks

Ammonia, min 99.5 wt %, water 0.5 max, oil 10 wtppm max — two storage philosophies for the same specification.

The storage condition is a design decision, and it is part of the design basis.
  • Pressure sets the vessel wall thickness and therefore the cost.
  • Refrigeration removes the pressure but adds a refrigeration package and boil-off handling.
  • Storage T fixes the pump NPSH and the feed enthalpy into your process.
  • Hazard class fixes spacing, bunding and relief requirements.
Always record storage T and P together: one without the other is meaningless.

Lecture 2-1 · 3. Feed and production rate

Capacity is quoted per year — you design per hour

$$\dot m\ \left[\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{h}}\right]=\frac{\text{Capacity [MTA]}\times 1000}{\text{operating hours per year}}$$

1 metric ton = 1000 kg = 1 tonne  ·  design convention in this course: 1 year = 8000 h (not 8760 h)

Where the number comes from

Company policy and market report; import/export statistics; company annual reports (e.g. market.sec.or.th).

Why not 8760 h

Turnaround, catalyst change and unplanned outages. 8000 h ≈ 91 % on-stream factor.

Feed vs production

State clearly which one the MTA refers to — they differ by yield and by recycle.

Lecture 2-1 · 4. Cost

Four problems with every chemical price you will find

It is not free

Current, reliable bulk prices (ICIS, Platts) are subscription products.

It is old

What you can access free is usually several years out of date.

It must be escalated

Adjust old prices to today with a price index (PPI, CEPCI).

It must be consistent

All prices in one economic analysis must come from the same date and the same delivery term.

Import/export statistics are a legitimate free estimate of the market price — provided you state whether they are FOB or CIF.

Which of these numbers is a design basis item, and which is a physical constant?
  • Critical T and P tell you whether the fluid can be liquefied at all at ambient.
  • Flash point and flammability limits fix the area classification and relief design.
  • Toxicity (LC50, TLV) fixes ventilation, spacing and detection.
  • The MSDS is the single document that carries all of it — use it to specify storage conditions.
Availability matters too: seasonal or single-source feedstock is a project risk, not a chemistry problem.

Lecture 2-2 · Cluster C

Space velocity: how a lab number becomes a reactor size

$\mathrm{LHSV}=\dfrac{\dot V_{\rm liquid}}{V_{\rm cat}}\ [\mathrm{h^{-1}}]$
Liquid feed — the most common for trickle beds
$\mathrm{GHSV}=\dfrac{\dot V_{\rm gas}}{V_{\rm cat}}\ [\mathrm{h^{-1}}]$
Gas feed, always state the reference T and P
$\mathrm{WHSV}=\dfrac{\dot m_{\rm feed}}{W_{\rm cat}}\ [\mathrm{h^{-1}}]$
Mass basis — independent of bulk density
A paper that reports only “WHSV = 2 h−1” without the feed basis or the catalyst bulk density cannot be scaled — ask for the missing number before you use it.

Casework · reading a real catalyst data sheet

Styrene by ethylbenzene dehydrogenation — what is still missing?

Data sheet as received

ServiceDehydrogenation catalyst
ComponentZnO–CaCO₃
ShapeCylindrical
Expected life1 year
DeliverySteel drum

Process note: styrene plants add steam with the feed. Steam removes coke precursors and lowers the ethylbenzene partial pressure.

List the reaction-basis items you still cannot fill in.
  • Stoichiometry & thermochemistry — the reaction is endothermic; $\Delta H$ is not given.
  • T and P — dehydrogenation runs hot and near atmospheric; no value stated.
  • Space velocity and the steam : EB ratio — nothing given, so the bed cannot be sized.
  • Catalyst size, bulk density — “cylindrical” is not a dimension.
  • Conversion and selectivity — equilibrium-limited, so a per-pass conversion is essential.
  • Poisons and regeneration mode — 1 year life, but replaced or regenerated in place?

Lecture 2-3 · 1. General

Capacity flexibility and overdesign

Plant capacity flexibility: process and off-site units shall be capable of continuous operation between 60 % and 100 % of capacity.
  • Units of measurement — one system for the whole project. Mixed kg/cm² and bar is a real source of accidents.
  • Project communications — language, document numbering, review cycle.
  • Sparing philosophy — which services get a standby machine.
1 kg/cm² = 0.9807 bar. Always state whether a pressure is gauge (g) or absolute.

Casework · past-exam style

Choose the cooling medium for a 5 MW condenser

The condenser must condense at 50 °C. Site data: dry bulb 35.2 °C, wet bulb 28.5 °C, cooling water 33 °C supply / 43 °C max return. Air-cooler approach 12 K; water-cooled approach 8 K. (a) Which medium works? (b) What cooling-water flow is needed for 5 MW?
Air cooler can reach35.2 + 12 = 47.2 °C
Cooling water can reach33 + 8 = 41 °C

Both are below 50 °C, so both work thermally. The air cooler leaves only 2.8 K of margin against the design dry bulb, and on a 42 °C ambient day it fails. Cooling water keeps 9 K of margin.

$\dot m=\dfrac{Q}{c_p\Delta T}=\dfrac{5000}{4.18\times10}$120 kg/s
Cooling-water flow≈ 430 t/h

ΔT = 43 − 33 = 10 K is fixed by the maximum return temperature in the design basis — not chosen by you. Exceeding it fouls the tower.

Answer: cooling water, on robustness. State the assumption (approach temperatures) in your design basis document.

Lecture 2-5 · Mental model

A language model is not a database

A model produces the most likely-sounding answer. Likely-sounding and correct usually coincide — and when they do not, the wrong answer looks exactly as confident.
  • It is excellent at structure, language, and orientation.
  • It is unreliable at exact numbers, prices, and citations unless it retrieves and shows them.
  • A fabricated reference is called a hallucination — and it is the single biggest risk to your report.
Use it as a research assistant, never as the source. The source is the document it leads you to.

WEAK

Lecture 2-5 · Academic integrity

Using AI honestly in your design project

Acceptable

  • Orientation: what routes exist, what to search for.
  • Explaining a concept or a standard to yourself.
  • Drafting and improving your own writing.
  • Setting up or checking a calculation you can reproduce.
  • Extracting tables from documents you supply.

Not acceptable

  • Quoting an AI answer as a source.
  • Citing a reference you have not opened.
  • Submitting generated text as your own analysis.
  • Pasting confidential or company data into a public tool.
You remain fully responsible for every number and every sentence you submit. “The AI said so” is not a defence — in this course or in professional practice.

Declare it

Add a short AI-use statement to your report: which tool, for what purpose, and how the output was verified. Example:

“Gemini and Claude were used to locate candidate literature and to draft the summary of the ethylbenzene route. All numerical data were taken from the cited primary sources, which were opened and checked by the authors.”

Transparency costs three lines and protects your whole report.

Lecture 2

Key Takeaways

1

Write the design basis down. Every number carries a value, a unit, a basis, a source and a date.

2

Design for the governing case, item by item — SOR or EOR, summer or winter, 60 % or 100 %.

3

Take the cheapest utility that works, and never assume one that is not on the list.

4

Rank evidence by closeness to your scale — commercial patent over lab paper, official statistic over guess.

5

Use AI to find, never to cite. Open the source, sanity-check it, cross-check it, log it.
