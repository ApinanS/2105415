How do you turn “which route is better?” into a number?

The situation

Same product. Four ways to make it.

Choosing by intuition is not engineering. You need a repeatable, defensible score.

The core idea

Route selection is exactly a rubric score

1 · Criteria

Which indicators decide? Yield, temperature, toxicity, cost…

Must be measurable at design stage.

2 · Score levels

How is each indicator turned into a number on one scale?

e.g. normalize everything to 0–1.

3 · Weighting

How important is each criterion relative to the others?

Experts, not the designer's mood.

Miss any one of the three and the “score” is just an opinion with decimals.

Step 1 of the rubric

What can be a criterion?

Reaction performance

Conversion and yield · reactant composition and concentration · reaction time

Operating severity

Reaction temperature · reaction pressure · heat of reaction

Materials handled

Need for a solvent · need for an inert · ease of purification (product distribution)

And always: environment, health and safety — plus economics.

Notation — keep it straight

Conversion, yield and selectivity

$$X = \dfrac{C_{A0}-C_A}{C_{A0}} \qquad Y = \dfrac{C_R}{C_{A0}} \qquad S = \dfrac{C_R}{C_{A0}-C_A}$$

For $A + B \to R$: $X$ counts what reacted, $Y$ counts what became product, $S$ counts how much of what reacted went the right way.

Textbook variant: some texts define $Y = \dfrac{C_R}{C_{A0}-C_A}$ and $S = \dfrac{C_R}{C_S}$ against a by-product $S$. Always state which definition you used.
Note $Y = X \cdot S$ — a high conversion with poor selectivity still gives a poor yield.

Step 1.3 — closing the data set

Gaps, conventions and where the numbers came from

“0” and “–” are not the same

0 = measured, no contribution (ODP, AP).
– = not found. Hydrogen carries 10 of the 25 gaps.

Three legal ways to close a gap

1. Use a justified surrogate — EC50 for a missing NOEC.
2. Estimate from a correlation — BCF from log Kow.
3. Drop the indicator for every route, and say so.

Never fill a gap with a zero. A zero is a claim of no impact; a gap is a claim of no knowledge.

Before you trust a property column

State the units, the test organism, the reference state and the year. Prices here are 2008 — old prices change the ranking of the economic indicator, nothing else.

Information gathering ends when every indicator you plan to use has a number for every route. Anything less and the comparison is not a comparison.

Step 2.1 — selection of indicators

Four groups, three sustainability dimensions

The safety group is the one built entirely from operating conditions — the numbers you already have in Table 5.

Step 2.2 — calculation of indicators

Normalize everything to 0–1 — and agree on the direction

Different units

°C, atm, %, kJ/mol, mg L⁻¹. You cannot add them.

Different ranges

Pressure spans 1–100 atm; conversion spans 0–100%. Raw sums are dominated by the big numbers.

One direction

Scale so that 0 = best, 1 = worst. Then the index can simply be averaged and summed.

Because 0 is best, the larger the final SCI, the less sustainable the route. This trips up everyone the first time.

Safety group — from condition to score

Temperature and pressure

$$\overline{T}_r=\begin{cases}1-e^{-(0.005\,T_r-0.125)} & T_r > 25\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}\\[2pt] 1-e^{\,0.020\,T_r-0.500} & T_r \le 25\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}\end{cases}$$
$$\overline{P}_r=\begin{cases}1-e^{-0.03(P_r-1)} & P_r > 1\ \mathrm{atm}\\[2pt] 1-e^{\,5.00(P_r-1)} & P_r \le 1\ \mathrm{atm}\end{cases}$$

Both curves are zero at the mildest condition (25 °C, 1 atm) and rise towards 1 as conditions get severe — in either direction.

Safety group — from performance to score

Conversion, yield and heat of reaction

$$\overline{X}_r=\frac{100-X_r}{100} \qquad\qquad \overline{Y}_r=\frac{100-Y_r}{100}$$

Low $X$ or $Y$ → large recycle streams and a complex separation train → a worse score.

$$\overline{\Delta H}_r = 1-\frac{1}{1+4.45\times10^{-5}\,\Delta H_r^{\,2}}$$

$\Delta H_r$ is squared: strongly exothermic and strongly endothermic reactions are both penalised.

Multi-reaction route? Take the condition giving the highest normalized value — the worst case governs.

Step 3 — integration of assessments

Where do the weights come from?

AHP

Analytic Hierarchy Process — experts compare groups pairwise, giving each group a weight.

DEMATEL

Decision Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory — captures how groups influence each other.

IWF

Combine both into one integrated weighting factor per group.

Expert opinion is required: relative importance changes with sector, country and company — it is not a universal constant.

Ethyl acetate — expert weights

Weights shift with the sensitivity factor $P$

$P$ raises the influence given to the safety group; the other three are rescaled to keep the total at 100%.

The headline number

Sustainable Cumulative Index

$$\mathrm{SCI} = \mathrm{IWF}_{ec}\!\cdot\! I_{ec} + \mathrm{IWF}_{e}\!\cdot\! I_{e} + \mathrm{IWF}_{s}\!\cdot\! I_{s} + \mathrm{IWF}_{oh}\!\cdot\! I_{oh}$$

The larger the SCI, the lower the sustainability of the route.

Within a group, indicators are averaged — equal importance by default. You may weight them individually if the context demands it.
SCI ranks and compares routes. It is a decision aid, not an absolute score: two studies with different expert panels will not give the same numbers.

Lecture 3-3

How do you apply the method to your casework?

Permission to simplify

You do not have to use all 21 indicators

Drop

Indicators that take the same value for every alternative. They add arithmetic, never information.

Keep

Indicators that actually separate your routes — those are the ones your decision rests on.

Add

Indicators your case demands and the published list omits: catalyst cost, ease of separation, water use…

The decision maker must select the indicators appropriate to the process — and justify the choice in writing.

Design casework — what is actually different?

Same feed. Same product. Same chemistry.

Identical across your alternatives: raw materials, product, and in most cases the reaction pathway itself.
Different across your alternatives: the catalyst, and therefore the temperature, pressure, conversion and yield it delivers.
Indicator groupDoes it separate your routes?
Economic (VA)No — same feed & product
EnvironmentalMostly no — same molecules
Occupational healthNo — same molecules
SafetyYes — conditions differ
So the decision indicator for a catalyst-selection casework is the safety indicator group: $T$, $P$, $X$, $Y$, $\Delta H_r$.

An honest limitation

What route selection does not cover

  • The route fixes the products and by-products — and therefore the upstream and downstream operations.
  • But the separation system is not among the route-selection indicators. It is invisible at this stage.
  • You may add an ease-of-separation indicator if product distribution differs strongly between routes.
  • Technology selection must be revisited after conceptual design, once the flowsheet exists.
Route selection narrows the field. It does not finish the design.

For a catalyst-selection casework, use the safety group: $T$, $P$, $X$, $Y$, $\Delta H_r$.

3

Take the worst case when a route contains several reactions.

4

Remember that indicators are coupled — you cannot improve one in isolation.

Casework · past-exam style

A fourth catalyst appears

A vendor offers a new catalyst: 240 °C, 3.0 MPa (29.6 atm), X = 96%, Y = 90%. Compute its safety index and decide whether it displaces Mo₂C/CNF (0.317).
$\overline{T}_r = 1-e^{-(0.005\cdot240-0.125)} = 1-e^{-1.075} = 0.659$  ·  $\overline{P}_r = 1-e^{-0.03(29.6-1)} = 1-e^{-0.858} = 0.576$  ·  $\overline{X}_r = 0.040$  ·  $\overline{Y}_r = 0.100$.
Index $=(0.659+0.576+0.040+0.100)/4 = \mathbf{0.344}$ — worse than 0.317. The 20 °C saving is more than cancelled by the higher pressure and the lower yield. Keep Mo₂C/CNF, and tell the vendor which two numbers would have to improve.

Casework · past-exam style

Audit this brief

“Design a plant producing hexadecane from methyl palmitate over Mo₂C/CNF at 260 °C and 2.5 MPa, conversion 98%. Feed is crude palm biodiesel.” Name three genuine gaps and the assumption for each.
1. Feed composition. “Crude palm biodiesel” is a mixture — methyl laurate to methyl linoleate. Assume the reported fatty-acid methyl ester distribution and treat methyl palmitate (45.1%) as the key component.
2. Hydrogen requirement and purity. HDO consumes 4 mol H₂ per mol ester; no H₂ source or purity is given. Assume 99.999% H₂ with a stoichiometric excess set by the designer.
3. Catalyst life and regeneration. No deactivation data. Assume a fixed catalyst charge of 0.025 g per 0.30 g feed with periodic replacement, and flag it as the largest uncertainty.
Not gaps: production rate, steady state, reactor pressure drop — these are your design basis.

Lecture 3 — the whole thing in five lines

Key Takeaways

1

Build a rubric before you argue: criteria, score levels, weights — then score every route the same way.

2

Normalize every indicator to 0–1 with 0 = best, so a larger $\mathrm{SCI}$ means a less sustainable route.

3

Keep only the indicators that differ between your alternatives — and say why you dropped the rest.

4

Test the ranking with a sensitivity sweep before you commit; report ties as ties.

5

Name every information gap and the assumption that closes it. Design basis is not missing data.
