Lecture 7-1

What must the reactor feed preparation system deliver?

Process structure

The first block every feed passes through

Reactor feed preparation stands between the storage tanks and the reactor, and it is the only block fed by both fresh feeds and the recycles.

Determination of process conditions

Every block takes its conditions from a neighbour

Reactor

Fixes what feed preparation must deliver, starting from the storage condition.

Purification

Fixes what phase separation must deliver.

Separation

Fixes both recycle stream conditions.

Design order runs backwards: fix the reactor first.

Scope of the block

Three jobs, and one thing you must know first

Adjust

Bring temperature, pressure and composition to the reactor’s requirement.

Mix

Combine fresh feeds with recycles.

Clean

Remove impurities that poison the catalyst.

Reactor conditions

Why the reactor is allowed to be this severe

The reactor sets the target. These five reasons are why the target is usually far from the storage conditions.

  • Convert reactant into product at all
  • Raise the rate, so the reactor shrinks
  • Hold a gas-phase reaction above its dew-point temperature $T_{DP}$
  • Improve selectivity to the wanted product
  • Shift equilibrium conversion in our favour

Lecture 7-2

Which rules fix the order of operations and the operating window?

General consideration

Four rules that fix the order

  • Change pressure, then temperature.A pump, a compressor or a valve changes the temperature as well.
  • Equalise pressure before mixing.Streams only join at one pressure; the higher one backs into the lower line.
  • Mix gas with liquid, then heat once.Two-phase heating needs far less area than heating a gas on its own.
  • Do not mix first when it is unsafe.No reaction at mixing conditions, and never air or oxygen with a flammable.

Overall heat transfer coefficient

A gas on its own is the worst thing to heat

Rule three looked like housekeeping. The overall heat transfer coefficient $U$ is why it is not.

Interactive · what U costs you

The same duty, ten times the area

$$A=\dfrac{Q}{U\,\Delta T_{lm}}\qquad Q = 13.7\ \text{MW (the HDA vaporiser)}$$

$A$ is area in m², $U$ the overall coefficient in W m⁻² °C⁻¹, $\Delta T_{lm}$ the log-mean driving force.

Heat transfer area—

Below about $U=100$ the area explodes. That is the whole argument for heating the mixture instead of heating the gas alone.

Work 30 – 50 °C below the maximum, or the fluid degrades quickly.

Above 400 °C only direct firing or electrical heating remain, and the choice must be justified.

  • Expensive equipment
  • Poor energy efficiency
  • Compounds start to decompose
  • Metal loses strength, so walls thicken

A heat transfer fluid does not remove the heater, it moves it. The fluid never touches the process — it gives up its heat across an exchanger, which is indirect heat transfer. Having cooled, it is reheated in its own fired or electrical heater. That heater is still on the flowsheet, one loop away.

Section summary

What 7-2 settled

  • Change pressure first, equalise pressure before mixing, then heat once
  • Mixing gas into liquid raises $U$ by an order of magnitude, and shrinks the area
  • Preferred windows: 1 – 10 bar and 40 – 230 °C
  • Outside those windows every degree is bought with a more expensive utility
7-3

Lecture 7-3

Which unit operation makes each change?

Unit operations for a chemical process

A plant only makes seven kinds of change

Feed preparation uses four

  • Change of pressure
  • Change of temperature
  • Change of phase
  • Mixing of fluids

The other blocks use three

  • Chemical reaction
  • Phase separation
  • Separation of chemicals

Naming the change first, then the machine, keeps you from drawing equipment you cannot justify.

How every unit is specified

One change each, one specification each

Pump or compressor change of pressure

  • Known inlet flow, temperature and pressure
  • Specify the outlet pressure
  • Determine outlet temperature, power, machine type
  • Ideal isentropic

Heat exchanger change of temperature

  • Known inlet flow and temperature, utility temperatures
  • Specify the outlet temperature
  • Determine utility flow rate and heat transfer area
  • Ideal no pressure drop

Vaporiser or condenser change of phase

  • Known inlet flow and temperature, utility temperatures
  • Specify the outlet vapour fraction
  • Determine utility flow rate and heat transfer area
  • Ideal no pressure drop, no heat loss

Drum or static mixer mixing of fluids

  • Known every inlet flow, temperature and pressure
  • Specify nothing — there is no free variable
  • Determine outlet temperature and phase
  • Ideal adiabatic, so enthalpy flows add

Change of pressure · liquid

Which pump for which duty

Use the centrifugal pump unless the duty says otherwise. Positive displacement is for high head at low flow, for metering, or for a slurry.

Interactive · why gas is different

Compressing a gas heats it, badly

$$T_2=T_1\left[1+\tfrac{1}{\eta}\left(r^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}-1\right)\right],\qquad r=P_2/P_1$$

$r$ is the pressure ratio, $\gamma=c_p/c_v$ the heat capacity ratio, $\eta$ the isentropic efficiency, taken as 0.75.

Mixing case 1 · liquid or slurry

Different components need an impeller

  • Two feeds enter through control valves; a level gauge holds the inventory and the vapour space goes to the vent system.
  • The jacket carries the service when the mixing releases or absorbs heat.
  • The stirrer is what makes this a mixing vessel rather than a drum.

On the flow diagram all of that collapses to one symbol: a vessel with an impeller.

Mixing a liquid feed with its own recycle

Almost the same liquid needs no impeller

The HDA drum mixes fresh toluene with recycled toluene. That is why it has no stirrer.

  • The two streams differ hardly at all in composition.
  • Mixing one component with itself needs no impeller — a drum, a pressure vessel or a static mixer will do.
  • Vertical or horizontal is a plot-space and residence-time choice, not a mixing one.

Mixing case 2 · low-viscosity liquid

Let the flow do the mixing

Fixed elements force the stream to keep changing direction. The turbulence that follows does the mixing, so there is no motor, no seal and no moving part to maintain.

Mixing cases 3 and 4 · gas involved

Once gas is in it, mix in the pipe

3 · Liquid with gas

Mix in line with a static mixer and carry the result on as two-phase flow.

4 · Gas with gas

No vessel and no static mixer. Two gas streams mix in the pipe on their own.

This rule produced S6 in the HDA case: joined in the line, heated as one stream.

Reference sheet · mixing

Every mixing symbol, and the variants

Two streams joining in the pipe need no symbol at all: a dot on the junction is the whole drawing.

Section summary

What 7-3 settled

  • Name the change first, then pick the machine
  • One specification fixes each unit: pressure, temperature or vapour fraction
  • Pumps are cheap; compressors are expensive and hot
  • Mixing rarely needs a vessel when streams are alike

Lecture 7-5

How is a process flow diagram actually drawn?

ISO 10628-1 · the international standard for flow diagrams

Six things, and nothing else

  1. Major equipment only — every vessel, machine and exchanger that changes the process, each with its tag and what it does
  2. The material flow between them, with direction shown
  3. The operating data that make the process understandable — temperature, pressure, flow at the points that matter
  4. Energy crossing the boundary: utilities, fired duty, refrigeration
  5. What every stream entering and leaving the diagram is
  6. Only the valves and fittings you need to understand the process

Items one and six together are the line between a PFD and a P&ID: major equipment, and only the valves you need to follow the process. A PFD that grows isolation valves, drains, relief devices and instrument loops has stopped being a PFD and become a bad P&ID.

Trays are drawn dashed, packing hatched and a catalyst bed crossed, so a reader can tell a column from a reactor without reading the tag.

Solids, and what every sheet carries

The last row is not equipment: it is the draughting that makes a sheet readable — crossings, arrowheads and the title block.

Rule 1 · layout

Left to right is time moving forward

  • Feeds enter at the left edge, products leave at the right, and the units sit along x in the order things physically happen.
  • A recycle is the one exception: it runs right to left, in a clear lane along the bottom of the sheet, never threaded back through the equipment.
  • Reserve that lane before you place a single unit.
  • Purge and vent leave at the edge nearest where they are made, not bundled into one corner.

Rule 2 · elevation

Use height only when it says something

  • Real elevation where a reader will reason from it: overhead system above the column, reflux drum above its pump, a pump below the vessel it draws from.
  • Separating parallel paths: gas high, liquid low, so the phases stay legible without labels.
  • Three units at the same height with nothing in common is a missed chance to say something — not a tidy grid.

Rule 3 · connections

Every line is orthogonal, and every arrow lands

  • Horizontal and vertical segments only. A line leaves and arrives perpendicular to the face it touches.
  • Arrowhead at the destination, tip on the outline. No floating arrows, no gaps.
  • At a crossing the horizontal runs through and the vertical breaks. One direction only, so any horizontal run can be followed edge to edge.
  • A line may cross another line. It may never cross through equipment, or run along a vessel wall.

Rule 4 · where the pipe attaches

Some symbols carry their meaning in the nozzle

  • Exchanger: process across, west to east; utility down the vertical axis. A process line stops on the circle and never runs into the coil.
  • Compressor: suction on the top of the wide end, discharge from the bottom of the narrow end.
  • Two streams combining meet at a tee in the pipe and the mixed line gets its own number. Two arrows into one nozzle says the vessel has two inlets.
  • Spread several connections across a face rather than stacking them.

Rule 5 · annotation and furniture

Where the words go

  • The tag goes under its unit, clear of every line. A tag floating to one side reads as a note about the pipe beside it.
  • A stream number sits on its line — never on a valve, an arrowhead, another number, or a crossing. If it has nowhere legible to sit, the layout is what needs fixing.
  • Number streams from 1, sweeping left to right and top to bottom.
  • Every sheet carries an equipment strip, a legend built from the symbols it uses, and a title block bottom right.

Section summary

What 7-5 settled

  • Six things belong on a PFD; a seventh makes it a bad P&ID
  • XX-YZZ A/B: class, area, item, spare
  • Left to right is time; the recycle lane is the one exception
  • Arrowheads land on the outline; the tag lives under its unit

Problem I · question 4 · the energy balance

How hot does the laboratory reactor get?

From the reaction sliden-butane ⇌ iso-butane,  $\Delta H_r=-9.29$ kJ/mol

$$n=\dfrac{65.79}{58}=1.134\ \text{kmol}$$n-butane consumed, divided by its molar mass
$$Q=1.134\times 9290=10\,540\ \text{kJ}$$exothermic, so this heat stays in the stream
$$\textstyle\sum m_i c_{p,i}=225.2\ \text{kJ/K}$$product masses times vapour heat capacities
$$\Delta T=\dfrac{10\,540}{225.2}=46.8\ \text{K}$$adiabatic, so no heat leaves the reactor

Exit temperature ≈ 197 °C. Valid only while the vapour heat capacities stay near their 150 °C values.

Problem II · the rest of the reactor information, and what it forces you to assume

Design basis and assumptions

  1. Single-pass glycerol conversion 95 %. On converted glycerol the selectivities are lactic acid (LA) 63 %, glyceric acid (GA) 22 %, complete oxidation 15 mol %.
  2. Reactor feed is held at 20 wt % glycerol, reached by diluting the 80/20 purified feed with deionized water.
  3. Glycerol decomposes at its normal boiling point, so vacuum distillation at 0.30 bara, where it boils at 235 °C, recovers it for recycle.
  • Basis: 60,000 t/y of the purified feed, so 48,000 t/y of glycerol.
  • Steady state, and item 7 means glycerol is recycled to extinction — overall conversion is 1.
  • Item 4 fixes the oxygen at the reactor, not at the process boundary.
  • Oxygen leaves through a vent, so the process excess is a result, not a choice.

Three mistakes that cost most of the marks

The dilution water

More than 80 % of answers never fed water to bring the reactor feed to 20 wt % glycerol — and 190,105 t/y is the largest single feed in the plant.

Which reaction sets the oxygen

The stoichiometric oxygen is taken on reaction (1) only, not on all three. The excess is quoted against that number.

The process excess

Assuming 0 % or 10 % excess at the boundary is wrong here: oxygen is vented, so the boundary excess is whatever the reactor and recycle leave — 216 %.

Problem II · question 5 · 20 marks

Design and draw the feed preparation PFD

Everything Lecture 7 taught, on one question. Name the change first, then pick the machine, then draw it.

  • The reactor wants 150 °C and 10 bara, and the water must stay liquid.
  • Air arrives at 1 bar, glycerol and water at 1 bar, and the glycerol recycle at 0.3 bar — below atmospheric, because it comes off a vacuum column.
  • Change pressure before temperature, and mix what is alike before you mix what is not.

Problem II · question 5 · the answer

From blocks to equipment, one unit at a time

Five changes, five machines — air up to 10 bar in a compressor, the three liquids mixed in a drum, the 0.3 bar recycle lifted by its own pump, the mixture pumped to 10 bar, then heated to 150 °C — and last the reactor they all feed. Pressure first, temperature last.

Lecture 7

Key Takeaways

1

Fix the reactor first, then design backwards to the storage tanks.

2

Change pressure, equalise, mix, and only then change temperature.

3

Give every unit exactly one specification, and let the rest be computed.

4

Specify vapour fraction, never temperature, at a change of phase.

5

Carry a liquid recycle above its vapour pressure or it will not stay liquid.
